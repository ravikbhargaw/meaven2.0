The Project Owner's Guide to Bare-Shell Transitions

May 14, 2026 • 6 min read

Handing over a bare-shell space to a dozen different contractors is the fastest way to lose control of your timeline and budget. For project owners, the transition to a business-ready office requires a different approach: central coordination.

The 'Bare Shell' Complexity

When you take possession of a bare-shell office, you're looking at more than just walls. You're managing electrical load calculations, data cabling layouts, HVAC ducting, and the critical sequence of flooring and partitions. For a project owner, managing these trades individually often leads to the "gap" problem — where one vendor finishes their work but the next cannot start because of a minor technical mismatch.

Why Coordination Beats Individual Hiring

Hiring the cheapest vendor for every trade seems logical on a spreadsheet, but it fails on the site floor. When the glass partition doesn't align with the floor spring because the flooring vendor didn't account for the pivot, who is responsible? In a multi-vendor model, they blame each other. In a coordinated model, the coordination partner owns the fix.

The 'One Partner' Value Proposition

At Meaven, we've shifted our focus to serve Project Owners directly. We act as the single point of accountability. Whether it's sourcing the right grade of aluminum or ensuring the pantry plumbing doesn't interfere with the server room's data lines, our goal is to give you a "One View" of your project progress without the construction headache.
